Book One: The Gate of Numbers

Chapter Five: Perfect Numbers
"The best way to understand mathematics is to try it yourself." — The Second Rule of Infinity Academy

The next morning, Ada carried one question with her all the way to school.

If prime numbers never ended...

...what other surprises were hiding inside mathematics?

She had begun looking at numbers differently.

Some belonged to families.

Some continued forever.

Perhaps every number had its own story.

When math class began, Mrs. Carter smiled as she wrote a single number on the whiteboard.

6

"Today," she said, "I have a mystery for you."

The class looked at the board.

"It's six," one student said.

Mrs. Carter nodded.

"It certainly is."

"But why might six be special?"

Hands immediately went up.

"It's even."

"It comes after five."

"It's half of twelve."

"My birthday is June sixth."

The class laughed.

Mrs. Carter smiled.

"Those are all true."

"But mathematicians think six is special for another reason."

Ada leaned forward.

She remembered what Mom always said.

Don't guess. Investigate.

Mrs. Carter handed each student a small sheet of paper.

"First," she said, "write down every factor of six."

Ada quickly wrote

1    2    3    6

Mrs. Carter walked around the classroom.

"Good."

"Now cross out the number itself."

Ada crossed out the six.

1    2    3

"Now add the remaining numbers."

Ada picked up her pencil.

One plus two equals three.

Three plus three equals...

She suddenly stopped.

1 + 2 + 3 = 6

Her eyes became wide.

"It equals six."

Mrs. Carter smiled.

"It does."

"Now let's see whether that happens again."

She erased the board.

Then she wrote another number.

8

Ada found its factors.

1    2    4    8

She crossed out the eight.

Then she added.

1 + 2 + 4 = 7

"Hmm..."

"It doesn't work."

Mrs. Carter nodded.

"What about ten?"

10

Ada listed the factors.

1    2    5    10

She crossed out ten.

Then added.

1 + 2 + 5 = 8

"Still not."

The classroom became unusually quiet.

Every student was testing another number.

Some whispered.

Some counted on their fingers.

Some erased and tried again.

Ada smiled.

Nobody had been given the answer.

Everyone was discovering it for themselves.

She looked toward the front of the room.

Mrs. Carter wasn't solving the mystery.

She was watching everyone else solve it.

Ada suddenly remembered the Second Rule of Infinity Academy.

The best way to understand mathematics is to try it yourself.

She smiled.

Mom would have liked this lesson.

Just before the bell rang, Mrs. Carter returned to the board.

"There is a name," she said, "for numbers like six."

She slowly wrote two words.

Perfect Number

Several students looked surprised.

Ada raised her hand.

"Can numbers really be perfect?"

Mrs. Carter smiled.

"That's an excellent question."

"I'll let you think about it."

The bell rang.

As everyone packed their backpacks, Ada looked once more at the word

Perfect

Yesterday... numbers had families.

Today... one number was called perfect.

Mathematics was becoming stranger every day.

That afternoon, Ada hurried to Mom's car before Mom had even turned off the engine.

"Mom!"

Mom smiled.

"I know that voice."

"It means mathematics happened today."

Ada laughed.

"We learned about something called perfect numbers."

"Oh?"

"The teacher said six is perfect."

"Did she explain why?"

Ada shook her head.

"She made us figure it out ourselves."

Mom nodded with approval.

"That sounds like a very good teacher."

Ada smiled.

"It reminded me of Infinity Academy."

Mom laughed.

"I was hoping you'd notice."

The rest of the drive home felt shorter than usual.

As soon as they arrived, Ada set her backpack beside the couch and followed Mom into the kitchen.

Mom opened the refrigerator.

Instead of taking out cherries this time, she picked up a bowl of grapes.

Ada smiled.

"More mathematics?"

"Of course."

Mom placed six grapes on the table.

🍇 🍇 🍇 🍇 🍇 🍇

"Yesterday," Mom said, "we used cherries to investigate prime numbers."

"Today we'll investigate perfect numbers."

Ada nodded eagerly.

"First," Mom said, "what are the factors of six?"

Ada answered without hesitation.

1    2    3    6

"And if we don't count the six?"

Ada smiled.

1 + 2 + 3 = 6

"It becomes itself."

"Exactly."

Mom nodded.

"So six is called a perfect number."

Ada looked thoughtfully at the grapes.

"It feels balanced."

Mom smiled.

"I like that description."

"Balanced."

"Let's see if another number is balanced too."

She wrote

28

Ada looked surprised.

"That's much bigger."

"It is."

"But don't be afraid of bigger numbers."

"We'll investigate one step at a time."

Together they listed every factor of twenty-eight.

1    2    4    7    14    28

Mom crossed out the twenty-eight.

"Now add the rest."

Ada worked slowly.

One plus two is three.

Three plus four is seven.

Seven plus seven is fourteen.

Fourteen plus fourteen is...

She suddenly stopped.

28

Ada stared at the paper.

"It worked again."

"It did."

"So twenty-eight is perfect too!"

Mom nodded.

"It is."

Ada leaned back in her chair.

"There can't be many of these."

"They're actually very rare."

Ada smiled.

"I like rare things."

"So do mathematicians."

For a few moments they simply looked at the paper.

Two numbers.

Six.

Twenty-eight.

Both somehow added back to themselves.

Finally Ada asked,

"Who decided they were called perfect?"

Mom thought for a moment.

"More than two thousand years ago..."

"...long before computers..."

"...long before America..."

"...there were people who believed numbers were full of beauty."

"They were called the Pythagoreans."

"They loved patterns."

"They loved music."

"They believed mathematics revealed the hidden order of the universe."

Ada listened carefully.

"They noticed that six behaved in a very unusual way."

"And later..."

"They found twenty-eight."

"So they called these numbers..."

Perfect Numbers

Ada looked down at the page.

"Do you think numbers can really be perfect?"

Mom smiled.

"I don't think mathematicians meant 'perfect' the way people do."

"They didn't mean that six never makes mistakes."

Ada giggled.

"That would be silly."

"I think they meant..."

"...that the number had a kind of beautiful balance."

Ada nodded slowly.

"I like that."

"It isn't perfect because it's better."

"It's perfect because it's balanced."

Mom smiled warmly.

"I couldn't have said it better myself."

After dinner, Ada opened her notebook.

On one page she had written:

Prime numbers never end.

On the next page she carefully wrote:

Some numbers are balanced.

Then she paused.

"If prime numbers are one family..."

"...and perfect numbers are another..."

"...how many number families are there?"

The question remained on the page.

Waiting.

The reading room was quiet that evening.

The warm light from the reading lamp spread across the couch.

Harry Potter rested beside Ada's notebook.

The piano stood silently against the wall.

Outside, the last rays of sunlight faded behind the trees.

Ada looked once more at the page she had written.

Prime numbers never end.

She turned the page.

Some numbers are balanced.

She smiled.

Mathematics no longer felt like a list of rules.

It felt like meeting new friends.

Each family of numbers had its own personality.

Prime numbers were mysterious.

Perfect numbers were balanced.

She wondered what other kinds of numbers were waiting for her.

Mom walked into the reading room carrying two cups of warm milk.

She handed one to Ada.

"What are you thinking about?"

Ada looked at her notebook.

"I think every number has a story."

Mom smiled.

"I think you're beginning to see mathematics the way mathematicians do."

Ada looked up.

"They don't just calculate?"

"No."

"They wonder."

"They notice patterns."

"They ask questions."

"They make mistakes."

"They try again."

"And sometimes..."

"They discover something nobody has ever seen before."

Ada looked back at the notebook.

She carefully wrote another sentence.

Every number has a story.

She stared at the words for a long moment.

Then she added one final question.

What story is my favorite number trying to tell?

She closed the notebook.

The room became completely still.

Tick.

Tick.

Tick.

The clock continued counting the seconds.

Then...

A gentle breeze drifted through the slightly open window.

Several books rustled softly.

One page turned by itself.

Then another.

The mysterious book on the highest shelf slowly opened.

Its pages flipped one after another.

Not quickly.

Almost as though the book were searching for something.

Finally...

The pages stopped.

A faint golden light spread across the paper.

Very slowly, a new picture appeared.

It was the same golden doorway Ada had seen before.

But something had changed.

A tiny symbol above the door was glowing.

It looked like this.

6

Beside it, another empty circle waited.

Then another.

And another.

There were many of them.

As if each discovery would one day light another symbol.

The doorway itself remained closed.

But it looked...

Closer.

Almost as if it knew Ada was coming.

Below the doorway, golden letters slowly appeared.

EVERY DISCOVERY BRINGS YOU ONE STEP CLOSER.

The words glowed for only a few seconds.

Then they slowly disappeared.

The page became blank once more.

The mysterious book quietly closed.

Across the room, Ada had already fallen asleep beneath the reading lamp, Harry Potter resting beside her, her notebook still open to the page that read:

Every number has a story.

Outside, the stars slowly filled the night sky.

Somewhere, far beyond anything Ada could yet imagine, the Gate of Numbers waited patiently.
