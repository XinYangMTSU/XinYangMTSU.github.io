Project 01

Self-Supervised Contrastive Learning for ASD Classification

Best starting projectCNN + SimCLRROI time series

Train a convolutional encoder on two augmented views of resting-state fMRI ROI time series. Then evaluate whether the learned embeddings improve ASD versus typically developing (TD) classification.

Research question

  • Can self-supervised pretraining produce more robust subject representations than a CNN trained from scratch?
  • Do learned features generalize across scanning sites?

Core methods

  • ROI time-series crops from each participant
  • Scientifically justified temporal augmentation
  • CNN encoder, projection head, NT-Xent loss
  • Linear probe or fine-tuned ASD/TD classifier

Evaluation & deliverables

  • FC + SVM/RF and supervised CNN baselines
  • Balanced accuracy, ROC-AUC, sensitivity, specificity
  • Site-wise and motion sensitivity analysis
  • Reusable PyTorch training pipeline

Important: Positive pairs are two views of the same participant. Split subjects before windowing; never let test subjects enter ordinary inductive pretraining.

Potential novelty: Develop motion-aware or site-robust augmentation strategies and test whether they preserve ASD-related information while suppressing acquisition artifacts. Demonstrate the gain through ablation studies.
Project 02

Graph Contrastive Learning for Functional Brain Networks

Intermediate difficultyGNN / GATFunctional connectivity

Represent each participant's functional connectivity (FC) matrix as a weighted brain graph. Train a graph encoder on paired views while preserving plausible network structure and functionally important connections.

Research question

  • Do contrastively learned graph representations better capture discriminative connectivity patterns?
  • Which graph perturbations preserve brain-network meaning?

Core methods

  • ROI-to-ROI correlation matrices as weighted graphs
  • Careful edge perturbation / subgraph views
  • GCN or graph attention encoder
  • Graph-level contrastive objective

Evaluation & deliverables

  • Compare FC-vector classifiers and noncontrastive GNNs
  • Ablate graph construction and augmentation
  • Assess robustness across parcellations and sites
  • Interpret network-level contributions cautiously

Important: Graph edges often represent signed correlations. Model how negative connections are handled; aggressive edge deletion can erase biologically meaningful structure.

Potential novelty: Design topology-aware, signed-connectivity-preserving graph views, and compare against random edge deletion under multi-site evaluation.
Project 03

Dynamic Functional Connectivity with Temporal Contrastive Learning

More ambitious directionTemporal modelingDynamic FC

Learn from evolving connectivity patterns rather than a single static FC summary. Use sliding-window connectivity sequences or temporal encoders to investigate time-varying brain organization and potential improvements in cross-site generalization.

Research question

  • Does temporal contrastive learning capture informative dynamic connectivity patterns?
  • Do those patterns generalize across sites and preprocessing choices?

Core methods

  • Time-resolved ROI signals or windowed FC sequences
  • Temporal CNN, GRU, or Transformer encoder
  • Positive-pair design across valid temporal views
  • Subject-level pooling or temporal summaries

Evaluation & deliverables

  • Compare against static FC, dynamic FC statistics, and supervised temporal models
  • Ablate window length and overlap
  • Estimate reproducibility and temporal reliability
  • Cross-site generalization experiments

Important: Sliding-window FC can be noisy, especially with short windows. Temporal fluctuations are not automatically evidence of distinct neural states.

Potential novelty: Develop reliability-aware temporal contrastive objectives that down-weight unstable connectivity patterns and test whether they generalize across scanners and acquisition protocols.

Background reading

Use the papers below to situate the projects in existing work. A paper that applies contrastive learning to fMRI is not automatically a novel research contribution.

  • SimCLR: Chen et al., A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations — foundational contrastive-learning design.
  • Resting-state fMRI graph learning: Unsupervised Contrastive Graph Learning for Resting-State Functional MRI Analysis and Brain Disorder Detection.
  • Graph diffusion augmentation: Self-Supervised Graph Contrastive Learning with Diffusion Augmentation.
  • Temporal contrastive learning: VarCoNet: A Variability-Aware Self-Supervised Framework.
  • Benchmark dataset: Autism Brain Imaging Data Exchange (ABIDE).
