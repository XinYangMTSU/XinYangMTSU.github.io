Localized Developmental Trajectories of the Arcuate Fasciculus in Autism

This webpage is a practical research-and-publication roadmap for paper 1 in the longitudinal autism neuroimaging dissertation sequence.

As autistic children grow, where along the arcuate fasciculus do microstructural developmental trajectories differ from typically developing children?

Scientific progression

Known: AF microstructure differs in autism.
Known: Longitudinal AF changes have been reported.
Gap: Whole-tract summaries can hide localized developmental divergence.
Contribution: Map where and when developmental trajectories diverge along the AF.

1. Paper scope

Keep this paper focused on one coherent biomedical/computational contribution.

Core contribution: Map where and when developmental trajectories diverge along the AF.

2. Specific aims

These aims should map directly to the Results section and major figures.

Aim 1

Reconstruct left and right AF longitudinally and generate along-tract FA/MD/RD/AD profiles.

Aim 2

Model localized developmental trajectories and test ASD × age effects along the tract.

Aim 3

Assess hemisphere, metric, site, and QC robustness and replicate where feasible.

4. Step-by-step implementation

Use this as the student's operational checklist.

Literature gap matrix

Document longitudinal and along-tract AF studies; define the exact novelty.

Dataset audit

Count ASD/TD participants with ≥2 usable visits; record age span and scanner/site.

Master table

Create one row per subject/visit with age, diagnosis, sex, site, motion, and QC.

dMRI preprocessing

Denoise, correct motion/eddy/susceptibility where possible, fit diffusion model, register to T1.

AF reconstruction

Segment left and right arcuate fasciculi consistently across participants and visits.

Along-tract profiling

Resample each tract to common nodes and extract FA, MD, RD, and AD.

Whole-tract baseline

Fit conventional mixed-effects models using mean tract metrics.

Node-wise longitudinal models

Test age, diagnosis, and age × diagnosis at each tract location.

Smooth spatiotemporal model

Use GAMM or functional mixed modeling to account for spatial dependence.

Multiple-comparison control

Use FDR, cluster-based permutation, or smooth functional inference.

Sensitivity analyses

Repeat with stricter motion QC, alternative age forms, and tract-profile resolutions.

Figures

Create developmental curves, tract heatmaps, effect-size maps, and left/right comparisons.

Interpretation

Emphasize spatially localized development rather than diagnosis classification.

Manuscript

Write significance, novelty, methods, primary figures, limitations, and replication plan.
