The big idea

Three questions drive the paper

01

Broad networks

Do the seven major cortical networks communicate differently in healthy controls, PD-NC, and PD-MCI?

02

Whole connectome

Do abnormal individual connections form a connected subnetwork that broad network averages could miss?

Shared PD pattern

Even if PD-MCI and PD-NC are not significantly different, do they show similar edge-by-edge changes relative to controls?

Steps 2–4 — Preprocessing & QC

Clean and align the scans before measuring brain communication

2

fMRIPrep preprocessing

For each participant, the pipeline uses a high-resolution T1-weighted structural MRI together with the resting-state BOLD fMRI. The structural scan is primarily an anatomical reference; the functional scan supplies the time-varying BOLD signals used later for connectivity.

Conceptually, fMRIPrep 25.2.5 performs several major operations: prepares the anatomical reference, extracts/segments brain tissue, creates a BOLD reference, estimates head motion, corrects susceptibility distortion when field-map information is available, aligns BOLD to the participant's T1 anatomy, and maps the data into a common MNI standard space. It also generates motion and nuisance/confound information used later in denoising and QC.

resting-state fMRI → participant's T1 anatomy → MNI standard space
3

Denoise and censor motion

The nuisance model includes motion regressors, anatomical CompCor components and cosine regressors. Frames are censored when FD > 0.5 mm or standardized DVARS > 1.5. Signals are detrended and low-pass filtered at 0.08 Hz.

4

Require at least 5 minutes of usable data

Each scan has 200 volumes and TR = 2 s. A participant must retain at least 150 volumes = 300 s = 5 min.

Why two kinds of MRI?

Structural MRI tells us “where”; fMRI tells us “how signals change over time”

T1-weighted structural MRI

A high-resolution 3D anatomical image. In this project it supports preprocessing: identifying anatomy, tissue segmentation, functional-to-anatomical alignment, and spatial normalization. We do not use cortical thickness or structural volume as outcomes in the paper.

Resting-state BOLD fMRI

A sequence of brain volumes acquired over time while the participant rests. These BOLD fluctuations provide the ROI time series from which functional connectivity is calculated.

Coregistration vs spatial normalization: coregistration aligns a participant's fMRI to that same participant's T1 anatomical image. Spatial normalization then maps that participant's anatomy into a common template space (MNI152NLin2009cAsym). This common coordinate system makes it possible to apply the same Schaefer-200 atlas consistently across participants.

Steps 5–7 — Functional connectivity

Turn each participant into a 200 × 200 brain network

5

Schaefer-200 parcellation

The cortex is divided into 200 ROIs, each assigned to one of seven Yeo networks: Visual, Somatomotor, Dorsal Attention, Salience/Ventral Attention, Limbic, Control, and Default Mode.

6

Extract 200 BOLD time series

After denoising and censoring, each ROI contributes one resting-state signal over time.

7

Correlate every pair

Pearson correlation measures how strongly two ROI signals fluctuate together. Correlations are Fisher-z transformed for analysis.

200 × 199 / 2 = 19,900 unique ROI-to-ROI connections
Output: every participant now has a 200 × 200 symmetric functional-connectivity matrix — a cortical connectome.
Steps 8–10 — Statistical experiments

Analyze the same connectome at three scales

A. Seven-network analysis

The 200 ROIs are summarized into 28 unique measures: 7 within-network + 21 between-network connections. Linear models compare all three group contrasts while adjusting for age, sex, and mean FD. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR controls the 28 tests.

B. Network-Based Statistic

NBS examines all 19,900 edges. Directional edge thresholds are t > 3.1 and t < −3.1. Suprathreshold edges are assembled into connected components, and 5,000 Freedman–Lane permutations test whether component extent exceeds chance.

C. Whole-connectome convergence

For every edge, the adjusted PD-NC–Control effect is compared with the adjusted PD-MCI–Control effect. Pearson/Spearman correlation and direction agreement quantify whether both PD groups share a broad PD-related pattern.

Take-home interpretation

What should a reader remember?

PD appears to involve distributed network dysconnectivity

The strongest pattern is not one isolated ROI or one canonical network. It is a connected set of altered edges spanning visual, sensorimotor, attention, control, default-mode and salience/ventral-attention systems.

Salience/Ventral Attention appears repeatedly

It appears among the strongest network-level effects, the largest NBS edge class, the highest-degree parcel, and most of the small set of shared component edges. This is a recurring descriptive theme, not an independently proven biomarker.

Static FC did not cleanly distinguish PD-MCI from PD-NC

That negative result matters. The two PD groups show moderate shared whole-connectome changes relative to controls, but the study does not support a strong categorical MCI-specific static cortical-connectivity marker in this modest sample.

One-sentence summary: The paper uses network averages, connectome-wide NBS, and edgewise convergence to show distributed PD-related resting-state connectivity alterations with recurrent salience/ventral-attention involvement, while finding limited evidence that static cortical connectivity alone distinguishes PD-MCI from PD-NC.

Deep-dive: how the three statistical analyses fit together

Analysis A — Seven-network analysis: Each subject's 19,900 Fisher-z ROI-to-ROI edges are grouped into 7 within-network and 21 between-network categories. Each category is the average of all Fisher-z edges whose endpoints belong to that network pair, giving 28 measures per subject. Linear models compare PD-NC vs Control, PD-MCI vs Control, and PD-MCI vs PD-NC while adjusting for age, sex, and mean FD. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR is applied across the 28 measures for each contrast. No measure survived FDR correction.

Why averaging can hide a pattern: a network pair can contain hundreds of edges. If only a subset is strongly altered, their effects can be diluted when all edges are averaged together.

Analysis B — Network-Based Statistic (NBS): NBS returns to the original 19,900 edges. For every edge, an adjusted regression produces a t statistic. Positive and negative directions are thresholded separately at t > 3.1 and t < -3.1. Surviving edges are joined into connected components when they share ROIs directly or indirectly. For PD-NC < Control, the largest negative component contained 102 ROIs joined by 142 suprathreshold edges. This means a distributed set of weaker PD-NC connections formed one connected subgraph; it does not mean every ROI or every edge is independently significant.

Permutation inference: 5,000 Freedman–Lane permutations generate a null distribution of the maximum component extent. The observed 142-edge component had directional FWE p=.0294. Because both directions were examined, the manuscript also notes a conservative two-direction interpretation of approximately .0588.

Analysis C — Whole-connectome convergence: For each of the 19,900 edges, the adjusted PD-NC−Control beta is paired with the PD-MCI−Control beta. Across all edges, Pearson r=.4652 and Spearman rho=.4461; 66.61% of edges had the same direction and 44.71% were lower than Control in both PD groups. The negative components shared only 7 exact edges but 42 ROIs, suggesting overlap in large-scale systems but heterogeneity in the exact strongest connections.

Combined interpretation: broad canonical-network averages did not survive correction, but the edge-level NBS revealed a distributed PD-NC-vs-Control pattern. The two PD groups nevertheless showed moderate whole-connectome similarity relative to controls. The study therefore supports distributed, heterogeneous PD-related connectivity alterations rather than a simple uniform change in one canonical network, while providing limited evidence for a distinct static cortical connectivity signature of PD-MCI.
