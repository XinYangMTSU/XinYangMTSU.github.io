Learning objectives

Explain how ICA differs from PCA, describe the linear mixing model X = AS, understand whitening and non-Gaussianity, explore FastICA source separation, and recognize scaling, sign, and permutation ambiguities.

Important correction: Two independent sources generally cannot be uniquely recovered from just one microphone mixture using ordinary linear ICA. This demonstration uses two mixtures from two simulated microphones. ICA requires identifiability assumptions, including independent sources and at most one Gaussian source.

Part 2 — ICA concepts and mathematics

1. Linear mixture model

The observed vector x is a linear combination of latent independent sources s:

x = As    and    ŝ = Wx

Here A is a mixing matrix, and W is an estimated demixing matrix. ICA generally cannot recover the original signs, scales, or ordering uniquely.

2. Whitening before separation

Center the observations and transform them so their covariance is approximately the identity matrix. Whitening removes second-order correlation; it does not generally guarantee independence.

3. Maximize non-Gaussianity

FastICA iteratively searches for directions whose projected values are non-Gaussian. A common fixed-point update uses:

wnew ∝ E[z · tanh(wᵀz)] − E[1 − tanh²(wᵀz)] · w

The result is normalized and, when extracting multiple components, decorrelated from other directions. The demo uses a simplified two-source FastICA iteration with a tanh contrast.

4. Interpret carefully

PCA seeks uncorrelated variance-maximizing directions; ICA seeks independent non-Gaussian components. Independence is stronger than zero covariance, and ICA's recovered signal order and polarity are not uniquely determined.
