Likelihood = p₁ × p₂ × (1-p₃). MLE wants this product as large as possible.

7. Why Log-Likelihood?

L = ∏ contributions → log L = Σ log(contributions)
Log turns multiplication into addition and improves numerical stability. Maximizing L and log L gives the same optimum.

8. Negative Log-Likelihood = Log Loss

Loss = -[y log(p)+(1-y)log(1-p)]

10. Search Across β₁ Values — What Does Each Bar Mean?

Each bar below represents the total likelihood of the entire four-observation dataset for one candidate β₁. It is not the probability of one observation.

Click Evaluate Candidate β₁ Values. Then click any β₁ button below the chart to inspect how that likelihood was calculated.
MLE rule: compare the likelihood produced by each candidate coefficient and prefer the β₁ with the largest likelihood. With this non-separable dataset, the likelihood rises and then falls, so students can see a clear finite optimum.
