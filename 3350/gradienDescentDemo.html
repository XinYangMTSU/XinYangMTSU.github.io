Gradient Descent

Do not memorize the formula. Build the idea yourself: prediction → error → loss → slope → update → repeat.

Step 1 of 9

Start with a real prediction problem

Suppose one student studies 3 hours and actually scores 9 points. We use the simplest model:

prediction = β × x

Here, x = 3 hours. β tells us how many score-points the model predicts per study hour.

If β = 2, what does the model predict?

This is where β comes from: it is a model parameter we want to learn from data.
Step 2 of 9

Prediction is not enough — measure the error

With β = 2, our prediction is 6, but the real score is 9.

error = actual − prediction = 9 − 6 = 3

What should happen if our prediction becomes exactly 9?

Machine learning needs a numerical way to say, “How bad is this prediction?”
Step 3 of 9

Turn error into a loss

A common choice is to square the prediction error:

Loss = (actual − prediction)²

Since actual = 9 and prediction = β×3:

L(β) = (9 − 3β)²

Now try different β values:

β = 1

prediction = 3
loss = (9−3)² = 36

β = 2

prediction = 6
loss = (9−6)² = 9

β = 3

prediction = 9
loss = 0

β = 4

prediction = 12
loss = 9
Now we have an optimization problem: find the β that makes the loss as small as possible.
Step 4 of 9

See the “bowl” of possible β values

You are at β = 2. Pretend you do not know the bottom is β = 3. What information could tell you which way is downhill?

Step 5 of 9

The gradient is just the local slope

For our loss:

L(β) = (9 − 3β)²

Its derivative is:

dL/dβ = 18β − 54

At β = 2:

gradient = 18(2) − 54 = −18

Gradient < 0

As β increases a little, loss goes down.

Gradient > 0

As β increases a little, loss goes up.
Gradient does not mean “the answer.” It tells us the slope where we are right now.
Step 6 of 9

Why subtract the gradient?

The gradient points toward increasing loss — uphill. We want downhill, so we go in the opposite direction.

βnew = βold − η × gradient

Let β = 2 and choose a small learning rate η = 0.01:

βnew = 2 − 0.01(−18) = 2.18

What happened?

β: 2→gradient: −18→move +0.18→β: 2.18
Old loss = 9. New loss ≈ 6.05. One small update made the model better.

Step 8 of 9

What does the learning rate do?

The gradient tells us which direction. The learning rate η controls how far we move.

Too small → slow. A useful size → efficient. Too large → overshoot or diverge.

One-sentence understanding: Gradient descent is a repeated process for adjusting model parameters so that the model's loss becomes smaller.
