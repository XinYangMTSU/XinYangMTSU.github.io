Step 1 of 10

What is a Graph?

Graphs are formal mathematical representations of networks and relationships. Objects are vertices (nodes); relationships are edges (arcs).

A graph lets us model social networks, roads, computer networks, airline routes, and many other systems.
Step 2 of 10

Adjacent Vertices and Adjacent Edges

Adjacent Vertices

Two vertices are adjacent if an edge connects them.

Adjacent Edges

Two edges are adjacent if they share a common vertex.

Example: edges (A,C) and (A,D) are adjacent because they share vertex A.
Step 3 of 10

Loops and Parallel Arcs

Loop

An edge connecting a vertex to itself.

Parallel Arcs

Two arcs with the same endpoints.

Important: a loop adds 2 to the degree of its vertex.
Step 4 of 10

Simple Graphs and Isolated Nodes

Simple graph: unweighted + undirected + no loops + no parallel arcs.

✓ Unweighted

✓ Undirected

✓ No Loops

✓ No Parallel Arcs

Isolated node: a node with no incident edges; degree 0.
Step 5 of 10

Degree of a Vertex

The degree of a vertex is the number of edges incident with it.

deg(v) = number of incident edge connections
For an undirected graph, degree is normally the number of connected edges. A loop contributes two connections.
Step 6 of 10

Complete Graphs Kₙ

Every vertex is connected to every other vertex by exactly one edge.

E(n) = n(n − 1) / 2
For K₅, E(5)=5×4/2=10 edges.
Recurrence: E(1)=0; E(n)=E(n−1)+(n−1).
Step 7 of 10

Paths, Length, Connected Graphs, and Cycles

Path

A sequence of adjacent vertices.

Simple Path

A path with no repeated vertices.

Connected Graph

A path exists between every pair of nodes.

Cycle

A closed path that starts and ends at the same vertex.

A → B → C → D
Step 8 of 10

Complete Bipartite Graphs Kₘ,ₙ

Vertices are divided into two disjoint nonempty sets. Every vertex in one set is connected to every vertex in the other set, but there are no edges within a set.

K₂,₃ has 2 vertices in one set and 3 in the other.
Step 9 of 10

Graph Isomorphism

Two graphs are isomorphic if they have the same structure under an adjacency-preserving bijection.
